**Paper**: [2611.31027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31027) — ComputeWise: Measuring and Fixing GPU Waste by AI Research Agents\
**Authors**: Egor Pakhomov, Nina Pakhomova, Anurag Koul, Shafiq Joty, Erik Nijkamp\
**Categories**: cs.AI\
**Reading scope**: Original arXiv v1 HTML, all eight main sections, four tables and five figures. The SVG table and figure assets were visually inspected. The experiments were not reproduced, and the benchmark implementation was not independently audited.

## The Problem

The assignment is to choose among nine optimizers for training a language model. A candidate must satisfy speed and loss conditions. In the paper's replay experiment, Claude Opus 5.5 runs all **50 training jobs**, gives the right answer after **19.0 hours**, and keeps the GPUs busy **93%** of the time (§1; Figure 1).

Yet early logs can rule out eight candidates within half an hour. Continuing their remaining trials adds work that this decision no longer requires. The paper's reference schedule finishes in about **4.0 hours**, while the pictured run using the proposed harness answers at **4.7 hours**. The reference has extra information, including which candidate passes, so its time is an explicit benchmark comparison rather than a freely available target (Figure 1; §§3–4).

Pakhomov and colleagues build ComputeWise to investigate this distinction. The question is how allocated GPU capacity becomes evidence for a supported answer. An occupied accelerator can be running a useful check, a trial whose outcome can no longer change the decision, or work that postpones a more important job.

## The Explanation

### Fix the research question and replay the experiments

ComputeWise has **nine research tasks**, including results tables, acceptance tests for memory-compression methods, optimizer selection and decisions about how many training repetitions are sufficient. Most give the agent four simulated H100 GPUs; one gives eight. Each recorded job uses one GPU (§3.3; Table 1).

The task selection matters. The authors screened **28 research designs** and kept tasks where no-help agents lost substantial time and simple reminders or a scheduler left waste behind. This is a diagnostic stress set. On their earlier mandatory job lists, where every requested run is needed, those simpler fixes often helped (§§3.1–3.2; Figure 2).

The agent's scripts still run CPU preparation. Matched GPU work then waits at the recorded pace and returns stored losses or scores. Agent deliberation and waiting take real elapsed time. No agent in the evaluation runs on physical GPUs; the replay fixes experimental outcomes so the study can examine decisions and timing (§4).

### Keep the timing rule with the score

The paper calls its normalized time a **reading**: the task's elapsed completion time divided by a specified comparison schedule's time. A reading of two means twice the comparison time. An answer must match the known key and have the required evidence to receive a valid reading (§4).

The clock's endpoint varies. The three results-table tasks stop at the last job. Optimizer-selection and acceptance-gate tasks use the later of the answer and its required supporting work; precision stopping uses the last answer turn, and data filtering uses the later of answer and last job. These differences remain part of the comparisons (Table 2).

GPU capacity is a separate account. The baseline study covers eight models with uneven task coverage. Its pooled account spans **148 runs** (§§1 and 5; Table 3):

| Use of held GPU capacity | Pooled share |
| --- | ---: |
| Needed busy work | 52% |
| Excess busy work | 22% |
| Idle capacity | 26% |

Busy time counts as needed up to the reference schedule's busy-work budget; busy time beyond that budget counts as unneeded. These shares account for held capacity, including some runs without valid readings. They do not partition the causes of elapsed delay. Some idle capacity is unavoidable even in a good schedule (§§4–5).

### Ask what each job can change

Two problems survive a full queue. A long required evaluation can start late, leaving other GPUs idle near the end. Or jobs can stay busy after a result has already disqualified their candidate. A scheduler can place submitted work on free devices without deciding whether that work should be submitted at all (§§5–6).

The proposed harness therefore begins with a plan. The model states the decision rule, lists candidates and labels jobs by their role. A **check** is cheap evidence that could reject a candidate. Further **needed** jobs wait for its checks to pass. The plan also names expected durations, dependencies, relevant log checkpoints and any planned interim statistical analyses (§7.1).

Code handles dispatch after that. It prioritizes runnable jobs that head long remaining chains, adjusts duration estimates from progress logs, and sends completed evidence to helper agents for judgment. The agents decide whether the research rule accepts or rejects a candidate; the program acts on that verdict by continuing or canceling work. Scientific judgment remains with the models (§7.1).

### A failed check can be the useful result

A second task shows the boundary concretely. A compressed-memory method must remain within **two score points** of an uncompressed model on every relevant workload. One check returns about **59.96**, compared with **62.42** for the uncompressed model, exceeding the permitted drop. The helper rejects the candidate. The program stops one running evaluation and drops two queued jobs, including an **8.1-hour** evaluation (§7.1; Figure 4).

That negative result contributes to the answer. Under an all-workloads acceptance rule, completing more evaluations cannot repair the already failed condition. In the optimizer example, the same principle lets a qualifying early log determine which later trials remain useful. The purpose of the job, rather than its occupancy alone, governs what happens next.

### Read the 82% as the statistic actually measured

The headline improvement concerns **summed normalized excess**, rather than all elapsed time. For each model/task pair, the authors take its median reading, subtract one, and count only positive leftovers. Across **39 pairs**, the summed excess falls from **31.3 to 5.6**, a reported reduction of **82%**. These summed ratios have no time unit; the percentage describes the decrease in that benchmark statistic (§7.3).

The implementations and task coverage differ, and the model groups improve unevenly (§§7.2–7.3):

| Tested group | Model/task pairs | Reduction in summed excess |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Three Claude 5.5 models in Claude Code | 26 | About 90% |
| GPT-6 Astra in the Codex setup | 9 | 63% |
| GPT-6.1 Sol in the Codex setup | 4 | 48% |
| Combined | 39 | 82% |

The Claude harness improves the reported median in **25 of 26 pairs**. Its 179 attempts yield 174 supported answers; the other five are handed back or end before the evidence is ready. The aggregate improvement therefore needs to be read alongside successful-answer conditions, task coverage and the comparison clocks (§7.2).

## Core Concepts

**Needed work is defined by the decision.** A short experiment that rejects a candidate can be necessary evidence. Further experiments for that candidate can become unnecessary under the stated rule, while remaining useful for another research question.

**Occupied capacity and completion time are different quantities.** The optimizer run keeps its GPUs busy while answering slowly. Capacity accounting describes where resources were held; the normalized clock describes progress relative to a particular comparison schedule.

**Research judgment and dispatch can be separated.** The model decides what evidence can settle the question. Code manages the resulting dependencies and timing. An incorrect plan or verdict can still undermine the execution.

## The Insight

Efficient research requires a plan connecting experiments to a decision. ComputeWise makes that connection measurable within its replay tasks. Keeping hardware active helps when useful work is waiting; deciding which work remains useful and when evidence is sufficient addresses another part of the delay.

## A Critical Reading

The nine tasks were retained specifically because waste persisted, and the harness was tuned on all nine. They share recorded sources and are not nine independent samples of research. The 52/22/26 profile is a description of this selected benchmark, while most baseline medians rest on only two or three readings. The work comes from known, one-GPU jobs; experiments invented during open-ended research were untested (§§3, 7–8).

The comparisons also have information advantages: some know job lengths omitted from the brief, and one knows the passing candidate. They are not proven optima, and an agent can finish below a comparison. Accordingly, a ratio above one does not by itself establish that the comparison's ordering was inferable at no cost (§§3–4; Table 1).

Simulation limits transfer. The hardware check covers **194 jobs with no agent**. Sharing-speed assumptions can alter threshold classifications, and a newer PyTorch version changes one acceptance decision. On five tasks, simulator version and experimental condition are partly confounded, although recorded job times are retained. The results do not establish the same gains for agents on actual GPU hardware (§§4 and 8).

Finally, planning remains a research responsibility. The controller cannot invent interim statistical looks without changing the experimental design; a fixed-sample design can be defensible. Two optimizer briefs can reasonably request every setup's mean, including work classified as unneeded. Long-job estimates and controller faults also leave delays behind. The method is evidence for a useful combined design to test, with these boundaries preserved (§§5–8).

## What You Can Use

Before launching experiments, record the question, candidate-rejection checks, remaining evidence, dependencies and valid stopping conditions. Estimate the long jobs and update those estimates from actual progress. For the optimizer task, an early disqualification should change the remaining plan; for an experiment requiring fixed-sample evidence, preserve that requirement. Measure supported completion and resource occupancy together, then test the design on the real workload and hardware.

## 问题

任务是在九种优化器里，选出适合训练语言模型的一种。
候选方案要同时符合速度和损失条件。
在论文的回放实验里，Claude Opus 5.5 跑完 **五十次训练**，**19.0 小时**后给出正确答案，GPU 有 **93%** 的时间在忙，见第 1 节及图 1。

可是，早期日志在半小时内就能排除八个候选。
继续跑它们剩下的试验，已经不能帮助这道题作决定。
论文的对照方案约 **4.0 小时**完成，图中采用控制框架的运行则在 **4.7 小时**作答。
对照知道更多信息，包括谁最终通过，所以它是明确的基准参照，不是免费可得的目标，见图 1 及第 3—4 节。

Pakhomov 等人因此做了 ComputeWise。
要检查的是：分配出去的 GPU 容量，怎样变成支撑答案的证据。
一张忙着的卡，可能在做必要检查，也可能在跑已经不能改变决定的试验，甚至把更重要的任务往后拖。

## 翻译

### 固定研究问题，回放实验过程

ComputeWise 有 **九项研究任务**，包括生成结果表、检查内存压缩方法能否通过质量门槛、选择优化器，以及判断训练重复到什么程度才够。
多数任务给四张模拟 H100，一项给八张；每个录制任务使用一张卡，见第 3.3 节及表 1。

任务是特选的。
作者筛查 **28 个研究设计**，只保留无帮助代理明显耗时、简单提醒或调度器仍留下浪费的题。
这是用于诊断的压力测试集。
在早期那些要求所有任务都必须完成的清单上，简单办法常能改善表现，见第 3.1—3.2 节及图 2。

代理脚本仍会真正做 CPU 准备工作。
匹配上的 GPU 任务则按录制速度等待，返回保存的损失或分数；代理思考与等待也占真实经过时间。
评价中的代理没有在物理 GPU 上运行。
回放固定了实验结果，使研究能集中观察决策和时间，见第 4 节。

### 分数必须连着时钟规则读

论文把归一化时间叫作「读数」：任务的完成耗时，除以指定对照方案的耗时。
读数二，表示用了对照的两倍时间。
答案还必须符合已知答案键，并有规定证据支撑，才得到有效读数，见第 4 节。

时钟终点因任务而异。
三项结果表任务计到最后一个作业完成；优化器选择与质量门槛题，计到作答和必要证据工作两者中较晚的时刻。
精度停止题计到最后一轮作答，数据过滤题计到作答与最后一个作业中较晚的时刻。
这些差别都属于比较条件，见表 2。

GPU 容量另算一笔账。
基线研究覆盖八种模型，任务覆盖并不均匀。
汇总 **148 次运行**，论文报告如下，见第 1、5 节及表 3：

| 占用 GPU 容量的用途 | 汇总占比 |
| --- | ---: |
| 必要的忙碌工作 | 52% |
| 超出的忙碌工作 | 22% |
| 空闲容量 | 26% |

忙碌时间在对照方案所需的忙碌预算内，记为必要；超过预算的忙碌记为非必要。
这笔账包括部分没有有效读数的运行，量的是被占用的容量，不能拿来拆分作答延迟的全部原因。
即使安排得好，也可能有不可避免的空闲，见第 4—5 节。

### 问清每个作业能改变什么

队列排满，仍可能有两种问题。
一个必要但很长的评测启动太晚，其他卡最后只能等它；或者，候选已经被排除，相关作业仍占着卡跑。
调度器可以把收到的作业放上空卡，却没有替研究者决定这些作业是否应该提交，见第 5—6 节。

新框架因此先要一份计划。
模型写清决策规则、候选方案和作业角色。
**检查**是能便宜地排除候选的证据；后续**必要作业**要等检查通过才运行。
计划还写预计时长、依赖、相关日志位置，以及预先安排的中途统计分析，见第 7.1 节。

之后由程序调度。
它优先启动后面还接着长链条的可运行作业，从进度日志修正时长估计，再把完成的证据交给辅助代理判断。
模型决定候选符合还是违反研究规则，程序按裁决继续或取消工作。
科学判断仍由模型承担，见第 7.1 节。

### 失败的检查，也可以是有用结果

另一项任务把边界展示得很具体。
一种压缩内存的方法，要在每个相关工作负载上，都与未压缩模型相差不超过 **两分**。
一次检查得分约 **59.96**，未压缩模型为 **62.42**，差距超过容许值。
辅助代理据此排除候选，程序停止一个正在运行的评测，取消两个排队作业，其中有一个要跑 **8.1 小时**，见第 7.1 节及图 4。

这个负结果对答案有贡献。
规则要求所有工作负载都过关，一项已经失败，再完成其他评测也不能补救它。
优化器例子同理：符合规则的早期日志，应该改变哪些后续试验还值得做。
决定下一步的，是作业对问题的作用。

### 82% 要按实际统计量解释

主要改善指标是**归一化超额的总和**。
对每个模型与任务的组合，作者取读数中位数，减去一，只累计正的余量。
**39 个组合**的超额总和从 **31.3 降到 5.6**，论文报告降低 **82%**，见第 7.3 节。
这些数是归一化比值的累加，没有小时单位；百分比描述的是这个基准指标的下降。

实现环境和任务覆盖不同，各组改善也不同，见第 7.2—7.3 节：

| 被测组 | 模型与任务组合数 | 超额总和降低 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Claude Code 中三种 Claude 5.5 模型 | 26 | 约 90% |
| Codex 设置中的 GPT-6 Astra | 9 | 63% |
| Codex 设置中的 GPT-6.1 Sol | 4 | 48% |
| 合并 | 39 | 82% |

Claude 框架在 **26 个组合中的 25 个**改善了报告的中位数。
179 次尝试里，174 次给出有支撑的答案，其余五次被交回或在证据齐备前结束。
因此，汇总改善要连着有效答案条件、任务覆盖和对照时钟一起读，见第 7.2 节。

## 核心概念

**必要工作由决定定义。** 排除一个候选的短试验，可以是必要证据。
该候选后续试验在这道题里可能已不必要，在别的研究问题中仍可能有用。

**占用容量与完成时间是两项量。** 优化器运行忙着，却答得慢。
容量账记录资源被占在哪里；归一化时钟记录相对于特定对照的进展。

**研究判断与调度可以分工。** 模型决定什么证据能解决问题，程序安排依赖和时间。
计划或裁决错了，执行仍可能受拖累。

## 洞见

高效研究需要把实验连到决定上的计划。
ComputeWise 在回放任务里，把这份联系变成可测量的东西。
有必要工作在等待时，让硬件忙起来有帮助；判断哪些工作仍有用、证据什么时候足够，则处理了延迟的另一部分。

## 博导审稿

九项任务就是因为浪费持续才被留下，框架也在全部九项上调过。
它们共享录制来源，不能当成九个独立抽样的真实研究任务。
52%、22%、26% 描述这个特选基准，多数基线中位数只来自两三次有效读数，见第 3、7、8 节。
工作来自预先已知、每项用一张卡的作业；开放研究中新想出的实验没有被测试。

对照还有信息优势。
它可能知道任务说明没给的作业时长，有一项还知道最终通过的候选。
这些不是已证明最优的方案，代理也可能比它更快。
所以，读数大于一，本身不能证明代理能免费推知对照的安排，见第 3—4 节及表 1。

仿真也限制推广。
硬件检查是 **194 个没有代理参与的作业**。
GPU 共享速度的假设会改变是否跨过浪费门槛，新版 PyTorch 也改变了一项通过判断。
五项任务中，仿真器版本与实验条件部分混在一起，虽然录制作业时长保持不变。
这些结果尚未证明代理在真实 GPU 上得到同等收益，见第 4、8 节。

最后，计划仍是研究责任。
控制程序不能擅自增加中途统计检查，否则会改变实验设计；固定样本量方案也可以有道理。
两道优化器说明能合理地理解成要求每种设置的均值，包括被算作非必要的工作。
长作业估计与程序缺陷也仍留下延迟。
因此，这是一种值得测试的组合设计，结论需要保留这些边界，见第 5—8 节。

## 启发

启动实验前，写清问题、排除候选的检查、剩余证据、依赖和有效停止条件。
估计长作业，并从实际进度更新估计。
优化器题里，早期排除应改变剩余计划；需要固定样本证据的实验，则保留那项要求。
把有支撑的完成情况与资源占用一起记录，再到真实工作负载和硬件上检验设计。
