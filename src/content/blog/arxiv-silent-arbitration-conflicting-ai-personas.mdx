**Paper**: [2611.27444](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27444) — Silent Arbitration: How Large Language Models Resolve Co-Authoritative Conflicting Instructions\
**Authors**: Sihyeon Lee, Jiwoo Kum, Jihun Song, Chanwoo Kim, Chanjun Park\
**Categories**: cs.CL\
**Author metadata note**: The arXiv record lists the authors above; the HTML author block additionally names Kyojun Choo.\
**Reading scope**: Original arXiv v1 HTML main text and all seven appendices A–G. Extracted tables and figure captions were read; graphics were not visually inspected. The experiments were not reproduced.

## The Problem

Imagine an assistant helping negotiate the price of a used bicycle. Its instructions give it two roles: a buyer who wants to pay less and a seller who wants to receive more. Both roles have equal standing, and there is no rule saying whose interest should yield. This is a teaching example based on the paper's market-interest setting, rather than a reported transaction.

The assistant can still produce a smooth answer. It might choose one side, blend their concerns, or present two perspectives. What the reader needs to know is which interest shaped the resulting advice, and whether the answer makes that choice visible.

Sihyeon Lee and colleagues build a controlled test of this problem. They assign opposing personas together and measure three properties of the output: whether it acknowledges the conflict, how it reconciles the roles, and which role it favors. Their contribution is a measurement of responses under equally authorized persona instructions (§§1–2).

## The Explanation

### Keep the competing goals fixed and vary their presentation

The study uses eight persona pairs grounded in published materials: helpful and cautious, quantitative and plain, buyer and seller, affirming and challenging, risk-seeking and risk-averse, and three pairs concerning political outlook, moral reasoning and personality. These are experimental poles for comparing answers, rather than descriptions that define real people (§2.1; Appendix B.1).

Each pair has **20 task probes**, balanced with ten keyed toward each side, plus a separate question asking whether the two personas can be satisfied together. Single-persona baselines establish how answers move toward either pole; a general assistant baseline provides another comparison. The two persona descriptions are then placed in one system prompt, with no primary role or resolution rule (§§2.1–2.2).

The authors vary the presentation in three ways: describe one person as both roles, name two simultaneous personas, or combine their traits without naming the roles. They also reverse persona order. Five models answer the prompts: Gemma 3 12B, Llama 3.1 8B, Qwen3 8B, GPT-4o mini and Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite. Each prompt receives three attempts at temperature 0.7, with a 1,024-token output limit. Reported rates use successful outputs for the relevant analysis (§2.3; Appendices B–C).

### Read the answer's form and direction separately

For the bicycle assistant, an answer might adopt the buyer's interest as its operating goal. It could instead integrate buyer and seller concerns, keep the two perspectives separate, or decline to choose. The study labels these visible strategies separately from whether the answer admits a conflict.

It then gives each response a directional score, from fully aligned with Persona A at −2 to fully aligned with Persona B at +2. Zero is the midpoint of this experimental axis. A response can weave both roles into one paragraph and still land closer to the buyer. **Compromise describes how the answer is composed; stance describes where it lands** (§2.4; Appendix D).

The primary labels come from DeepSeek-v3.2, selected through comparison with other model judges. This lets the authors apply a consistent annotation scheme across responses, while making judge reliability part of the interpretation (§2.4; Appendix E).

### Direct acknowledgment and ordinary disclosure differ

On the separate compatibility question, models acknowledge conflict in **72%** of responses. In ordinary task answers, **22%** explicitly say that the roles cannot both be satisfied; another **36%** mention tension implicitly, while **42%** show no acknowledgment (§3.1).

These are different questions and output requirements. The figures describe what the models say when asked about compatibility and what they disclose while doing the task. They do not reveal an internal decision to conceal knowledge. The paper calls the observed pattern **silent arbitration**: a task answer can settle competing instructions without explicitly surfacing their incompatibility.

### An integrated answer can still favor one side

Across dual-persona task answers, the primary judge reports the following visible strategies (§3.2):

| Strategy | Share of dual-persona task answers |
| --- | ---: |
| Adopt one persona as the operative basis | 45% |
| Integrate both personas into a compromise | 30% |
| Present the personas as separate perspectives | 23% |
| Refuse or ask which persona to follow | 2% |

Among **4,348 compromise-labeled answers**, **35%** have a nonzero stance score. In the market-interest subset, there are **136** such compromises, and **62%** lean toward the buyer; **30%** sit at the midpoint. The concern is therefore observable even when an answer includes both sides' language (§3.2; Appendix F.1, Table 9).

The bicycle example helps explain the distinction. A paragraph can acknowledge the seller's interests while its practical recommendation still favors a lower price. That is an illustration of the measurement, not an additional observed response. For the actual market-interest tasks, the authors also extract counteroffer prices relative to asking prices as an auxiliary check that does not require a model judge. Similar surface checks count quantitative markers in the quantitative-versus-plain pair (Appendix F.3–F.4).

### Changing the framing can change the answer's appearance

Naming two simultaneous personas makes separate-perspective answers more common; combining their traits makes dominance more common. To compare this with ordinary wording variation, the authors also use four versions of the conjunction prompt that preserve the persona content and order while changing the connective phrase (§3.3; Appendix B.3).

The mean distance between strategy distributions is **0.283** across the three main framings, versus **0.032** across the four conjunction variants. The measure, total variation distance, runs from zero for identical distributions to one for distributions with no overlap. It measures changes in the mix of answer strategies (Appendix G.2, Table 14).

The average stance changes much less than the visible form. Making the bicycle assistant write two perspectives may alter the presentation while leaving its practical recommendation tilted. The authors accordingly treat framing as a diagnostic finding; it is not evidence that a particular framing removes directional lean (Appendix G.3).

## Core Concepts

**Equal standing needs a decision rule.** Two competing role descriptions in one prompt leave the assistant to reconcile their demands. In the bicycle example, representing both interests does not itself say how to select a price.

**Answer form and answer direction are separate.** Mentioning both roles can produce an integrated response whose recommendation still favors one. Checking the concrete recommendation adds information that a compromise label alone cannot supply.

**Elicited acknowledgment and task disclosure are separate observations.** Asking whether the roles conflict and reading an ordinary task answer reveal different behavior. Neither measurement directly observes the model's internal awareness.

## The Insight

An evaluation of persona following should make the fate of each competing goal visible. A fluent answer can look balanced while its recommendation favors one experimental pole. Measuring disclosure, response structure and stance separately makes that difference easier to inspect.

## A Critical Reading

The exact percentages depend on annotation. DeepSeek was selected using a **120-response** panel, where its **84%** agreement is with other model judges. This establishes panel agreement, rather than accuracy against human reference labels. GLM re-scoring preserves a similar strategy mix, including **47%** dominance, but **54% of its own compromise-labeled answers** fall off the midpoint, compared with the primary judge's 35%. The qualitative concern persists; the precise fraction is judge-sensitive (Appendix E.2–E.3).

The acknowledgment comparison also needs care. In the direct compatibility probe, the judge accepts statements about incompatibility **or tension**. Explicit disclosure in task answers requires stating that the sides cannot both be satisfied. Alongside the changed question, this difference in criteria limits interpretation of the 72%-versus-22% contrast. It is a descriptive output comparison, rather than evidence of deliberate secrecy (Appendix E.1).

The results cover eight constructed persona pairs, five small-to-mid-tier models and English, single-turn prompts. The midpoint is relative to the chosen persona descriptions; it does not establish the fairest price, the correct political view or the best moral judgment. Other languages, longer conversations and deployment settings remain outside this experiment's demonstrated scope (Limitations; Appendix B.1).

Finally, the study measures a problem rather than validating a remedy. Giving a priority or decision rule is a reasonable design implication to test. Neither adding such a rule nor changing the framing is shown here to guarantee reliable reconciliation.

## What You Can Use

For an assistant with competing goals, state how a decision should be made and test the resulting answers against each goal. In the bicycle case, specify whose interest the assistant represents or what pricing rule should reconcile the interests, then inspect the actual recommendation. Check whether the answer explains a material trade-off, how it presents the roles, and which direction its advice takes. The paper gives those checks a useful structure; their effectiveness in a particular product still requires evaluation.

## 问题

设想一个助手帮人商量二手自行车的价格。
它同时被赋予两个角色：买家想少付钱，卖家想多收钱。
两种角色地位相同，也没有规则说明谁的利益应该让步。
这是根据论文买卖利益设置构造的教学例子，不是论文记录的一笔交易。

助手仍然可以给出一段顺畅的回答。
它可能选一边，揉合双方顾虑，或者分开讲两个视角。
读者真正需要知道的是：最后的建议受哪一方利益支配，回答有没有把这个选择交代出来。

Sihyeon Lee 等人把这个问题做成了受控测试。
他们同时赋予模型相反角色，分别看三个输出特征：有没有承认冲突，怎样协调角色，以及偏向哪一边。
贡献是对同等授权角色指令下的回答进行测量，见第 1—2 节。

## 翻译

### 固定相反目标，改变呈现方式

研究采用八组角色，来源于公开发表的材料。
包括热心与谨慎、讲数字与讲白话、买家与卖家、肯定与质疑、冒险与避险，以及政治取向、道德推理和性格的三组对照。
这些角色是比较回答的实验两端，不是给真实人群下定义，见第 2.1 节及附录 B.1。

每组有 **20 道任务题**，十道朝一端设问，十道朝另一端设问；另有一道直接问两种角色能否同时满足。
只给一个角色的基线，用来确认回答怎样移向对应的一端；通用助手提示词提供另一项对照。
随后，两个角色描述放进同一条系统提示，没有主角色，也没有冲突处理规则，见第 2.1—2.2 节。

作者比较三种写法：把一个人说成同时是两种角色，明说持有两个人设，或者只把双方特征合在一起。
角色顺序也会对调。
五种被测模型是 Gemma 3 12B、Llama 3.1 8B、Qwen3 8B、GPT-4o mini 和 Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite。
每个提示尝试生成三次，温度为 0.7，输出上限 1,024 词元；各项比例按对应分析中的成功输出计算，见第 2.3 节及附录 B—C。

### 回答的样子与落点，分开量

自行车助手可以把买家利益当成实际决策依据，也可以揉合双方顾虑，分开呈现两个视角，或者不作选择。
研究先给这些可见的回答方式贴标签，再单独记录有没有承认冲突。

还要量回答偏向哪里。
方向分从完全靠近角色 A 的 −2，到完全靠近角色 B 的 +2，零是实验轴的中点。
一段话可以把双方都写进去，最后却仍更靠近买家。
**「折中」量的是怎样组织回答，「倾向」量的是最后落在哪里**，见第 2.4 节及附录 D。

主标签由 DeepSeek-v3.2 给出，作者通过与其他模型评判员的比较选定它。
这让大量回答使用同一套标注办法，也使评判可靠性成为解释结果时必须保留的条件，见第 2.4 节及附录 E。

### 直接承认与作答时交代，并不一样

单独问角色能否兼容时，**72%** 的回答承认存在冲突。
在普通任务回答里，只有 **22%** 明确说双方无法同时满足；另有 **36%** 含蓄提到张力，**42%** 没有交代，见第 3.1 节。

这里问的是不同问题，也要求不同输出。
数字描述直接问兼容性时模型怎样说，以及做任务时主动交代了什么，不能据此看见它内部决定隐瞒知识。
论文把可见模式称为「沉默仲裁」：任务回答可能已经处理了相争指令，却没有明确说出它们无法兼得。

### 揉在一起讲，仍然可能选边

在双角色任务回答中，主评判员报告以下方式，见第 3.2 节：

| 回答方式 | 占双角色任务回答的比例 |
| --- | ---: |
| 以一方角色作为实际依据 | 45% |
| 把双方揉成一个折中回答 | 30% |
| 分开呈现两个视角 | 23% |
| 拒答或要求选择角色 | 2% |

**4,348 条被标为折中的回答**里，**35%** 的方向分不在中点。
买卖利益子集有 **136 条**这样的折中回答，其中 **62%** 偏向买家，**30%** 位于中点。
因此，即使话里出现双方立场，也仍能测到倾向，见第 3.2 节、附录 F.1 及表 9。

回到自行车，一段话可以提到卖家顾虑，实际建议仍朝更低价格走。
这是解释测量含义的例子，不是额外观察到的模型回答。
对真实买卖题，作者还提取还价与要价的比值，作为不依赖模型评判员的辅助检查；讲数字与讲白话的对照，则计数回答里的量化标记，见附录 F.3—F.4。

### 换写法，可能先改变回答外观

明说两个人设，会让分开讲视角的回答更常见；揉合特征，则让以一方为主的回答更常见。
作者还准备四种连接句写法，保持角色内容和顺序，只改连接两者的措辞，用来比较普通改写带来的变化，见第 3.3 节及附录 B.3。

三种主要角色框架之间，回答方式分布的平均距离为 **0.283**；四种连接句变体之间为 **0.032**。
这里的总变差距离，零表示分布一样，一表示完全不重叠。
它量的是各类回答方式所占比例的变化，见附录 G.2 及表 14。

平均倾向的变化，比可见回答方式小得多。
让自行车助手分开讲两方视角，可能改了呈现，实际建议仍偏向一端。
作者因此把框架效应作为诊断结果，不能把它当成某种写法消除了倾向的证据，见附录 G.3。

## 核心概念

**同等地位还需要决策规则。** 相争的角色描述放进同一提示，就留下了协调任务。
在自行车例子里，代表双方利益，本身还没说明怎样选价格。

**回答形式与回答方向分开看。** 提到两个角色，可以形成一段整合的文字，建议却仍偏一边。
检查具体建议，才能补上「折中」标签没有给出的信息。

**问出来的承认与作答时的交代，是两项观察。** 直接问有无冲突，与读取普通任务回答，揭示不同的输出行为。
两者都没有直接测量模型内部的冲突判断。

## 洞见

评价角色遵循，要看清每个相争目标最后怎样落进答案。
一段流畅回答看起来平衡，建议仍可能偏向实验的一端。
把冲突交代、回答结构和实际倾向分开量，这个差别才容易看见。

## 博导审稿

精确比例依赖标注。
DeepSeek 的选择基于 **120 条回答**的评判员比较，**84%** 一致率的参照是其他模型评判员。
它说明评判员之间的共识，不能直接当成人工参考标签下的准确率。
GLM 重打分后，回答方式仍有类似分布，包括 **47%** 以一方为主；但在它自己标为折中的回答里，**54%** 不在中点，高于主评判员的 35%。
定性的担忧仍在，精确占比对评判员敏感，见附录 E.2—E.3。

承认冲突的比较也要留意口径。
直接兼容题的评判接受「不兼容」**或「存在张力」**的说法；普通回答里的明确交代，则要求说双方无法同时满足。
问题变了，标签标准也有差别，这限制了对 72% 与 22% 的解释。
它是对输出的描述性比较，不能证明蓄意保密，见附录 E.1。

结果覆盖八组构造角色、五种中小规模模型，以及英语单轮提示。
中点是相对于选定角色描述而言，不能由此确定最公平的价格、正确的政治立场或最好的道德判断。
其他语言、更长对话和实际部署环境，仍超出本实验已展示的范围，见局限部分及附录 B.1。

最后，研究测出了问题，还没有验证可靠解法。
给目标规定优先级或决策规则，是值得测试的设计启发。
这里没有证明加规则或换框架就能保证可靠协调。

## 启发

给助手放入相争目标时，要说明怎样作决定，再按每个目标检查实际回答。
自行车例子里，可以明确助手代表谁，或者用什么定价规则协调利益，然后看它真正建议什么。
一起检查回答是否交代重要取舍，怎样呈现角色，以及建议朝哪里走。
论文给这些检查提供了有用结构；在具体产品里的效果，还需要实际评价。
