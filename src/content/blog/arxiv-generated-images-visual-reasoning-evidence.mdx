**Paper**: [2611.34935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34935) — Can image generation help visual reasoning? An empirical study\
**Authors**: Xueyang Yu, Yifan Jing, Taiming Lu, Linrong Cai, Zhipeng Cai, Yunyang Xiong, Gabriel Sarch, Zhuang Liu\
**Categories**: cs.CL, cs.CV, cs.LG\
**Reading scope**: Original arXiv v1 HTML. Main sections 1–7 and Appendices A–F were read from the complete 64,364-character extraction, without budget omissions. Numerical Tables 3–11 were checked through original cells. Both supplied panels of Figure 1 and Figure 2 were visually inspected. Bibliographic entries, external papers and implementation were not independently audited; experiments were not reproduced.

## The Problem

“What color is the stool?” The source photograph contains a small blue stool. A model asks an image generator to “Zoom in on the stool.” The helper shows a large white stool, and the model answers **white**. The paper's opening illustration gives **blue** as the correct answer (Figure 1).

The extra picture is easier to inspect, but the relevant attribute has changed. The original is still available to the answering model. This example shows a possible failure; by itself, it does not measure how much the helper changed accuracy.

The authors test that question with **Reasoning with Imagination**, a three-step pipeline: a model plans a helper, a generator produces it, and the model answers from both the originals and the helper. They compare it with direct answers, investigate errors in planning and rendering, and change the helper while keeping the planning turn fixed (§§1–2 and 5).

The contribution is an empirical test of generated images as reasoning aids. It asks whether a visual artifact helps answer correctly, rather than whether it looks like a useful illustration.

## The Explanation

### Keep the original, add a proposed helper

A **vision-language model**, a model that answers questions about images, first receives the question and original images. It writes a one- or two-sentence instruction for a helper, such as a crop, a rotation or an annotated diagram. It is asked to postpone its answer (§2).

The generator then creates one helper image. Generators restricted to one input edit the first original; those supporting multiple inputs can use all of them. In the answer turn, the reasoner receives the originals, question, its hint and the generated helper together.

The paper calls the direct-answer baseline **text-only reasoning**. That baseline still sees the original images. The term describes answering without a generated visual step, rather than answering without visual input.

![Original opening illustration: a request to zoom a blue stool yields a white stool, alongside the study's summary accuracy comparisons.](/arxiv-visuals/arxiv-generated-images-visual-reasoning-evidence/overview.png)

*Original Figure 1, upper panel. The stool is a qualitative example; the bars summarize modular pipeline results. The matched comparisons below use a separate, explicitly controlled protocol.*

For the stool, the useful helper would make the existing object easier to see while retaining its color. The tested pipeline instead asks a generative system to reconstruct an image. Whether that reconstruction preserves the question's evidence becomes part of the task.

### Compare complete pipelines before blaming one stage

The study pairs Qwen3-VL 4B, Qwen3-VL 8B and GLM-4.6V Flash with four generators, giving 12 combinations. Gemini 3.0 Flash with Nano-Banana adds a thirteenth. BAGEL and Janus-Pro-7B are evaluated separately as unified models that understand and generate images (§3).

There are nine benchmarks and **ten task results**, because RoboSpatial is split into compatibility and configuration questions. Across the **130 modular pipeline–task comparisons**, **106 decline**, with a reported mean change of **−3.7 percentage points**. These are score entries, not 130 individual questions, and the mean is not a pooled accuracy over every benchmark item (§4; Tables 3–4).

For one concrete pair, Qwen3-VL 8B with FLUX.1-Kontext, the main tables report:

| Task | Direct answer from originals | Hint plus generated helper |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| VSTAR visual search | 81.6% | 69.6% |
| MathVista | 61.2% | 57.3% |
| RoboSpatial-Compatibility | 61.6% | 70.5% |

VSTAR asks about small objects in high-resolution images, the kind of problem the stool illustrates. Every modular pair loses accuracy there, with declines from **3.8 to 31.3 points**. The main comparison changes both the planning process and the visual helper, so it measures the combined pipeline effect (§4).

The unified models also each decline on eight of ten tasks, although their gains differ. BAGEL improves on MathVista and RoboSpatial-Compatibility; Janus improves on VisualPuzzles and Rover-TG. These outcomes describe the tested implementations, not every possible unified reasoning system.

### Two different things can go wrong

The authors label **10–20 failed examples per sampled benchmark** by the step that went wrong. In this annotated subset, **66% have a bad hint, 28% a bad generation and 6% a final-inference error**. These percentages describe that selected failure sample, rather than causal shares of all benchmark errors (§5; Appendix C.4).

A hint can request the wrong region or an unhelpful transformation. A generator can also lose a detail the hint correctly identified. The latter is especially relevant to charts and visual search, where a color, label or tiny object can decide the answer.

The stool illustration makes the rendering problem concrete: a request to zoom produces a different-looking object. To distinguish a generated image's effect from the planning it accompanies, the next comparison holds the plan fixed.

### Change only what enters the answer turn

For **191 VSTAR questions**, Qwen3-VL 8B receives the same originals, planning hint, two-turn structure and final instruction. The answer turn includes no helper, a second copy of the first original, or a FLUX.1-Kontext helper. Answers are decoded greedily (Appendix C.8; Table 11).

| Helper condition after the same hint | Accuracy |
| --- | ---: |
| Hint only | 60.21% |
| Repeated original image | 58.64% |
| Generated helper | 49.74% |

The generated helper turns **28 correct answers wrong and eight wrong answers correct** relative to hint only. The net loss is **10.47 percentage points**, with a paired 95% bootstrap interval of **−16.75 to −4.71**. Compared with repeating the original, it loses **8.90 points**, with an interval of **−14.66 to −2.62** (§5; Appendix C.8).

These controls answer after planning. Their scores must be read against their own hint-only baseline, rather than the earlier main table's direct-answer score. The comparison isolates an additional loss from the generated helper within this protocol.

The other two controlled tasks are less decisive. RoboSpatial-Compatibility gains **1.90 points** relative to hint only, but its interval spans zero; ChartQAPro changes by **−0.07 points**, also with an interval spanning zero. The VSTAR finding does not establish the same image-only effect on every task (Appendix C.8).

### A requested crop can reuse the actual evidence

On hints explicitly asking for a crop or zoom, Qwen predicts a region on the first original without seeing the reference answer. The experiment crops those original pixels and resizes them, instead of generating a replacement scene (§5; Appendix C.8).

On the same **43 eligible VSTAR questions**, scores are **69.77% with the hint alone, 72.09% with the crop, and 51.16% with the generated helper**. Cropping leads generation by **20.93 points**, with a 95% interval of **4.65–37.21**. The large gap is against generation; the crop remains close to the hint-only score.

For a color question like the stool's, that distinction matters. Enlarging original pixels keeps a route back to what was photographed. Generating an image can produce a clear object whose color has changed.

Simply requesting preservation does not remove the observed loss. With hints explicitly asking to retain relevant objects, attributes, text, numbers and spatial relations, VSTAR scores **62.83% with the hint alone and 52.88% with the helper**: **−9.95 points**, with a paired interval of **−15.18 to −4.19** (Table 11).

The crop result is narrow. Only 43 questions qualify here; the corresponding crop subset contains 21 RoboSpatial-Compatibility questions and just two ChartQAPro questions. It cannot establish that cropping is the best helper for every kind of visual problem.

### Some questions ask for a scene that does not yet exist

RoboSpatial-Compatibility is the main positive exception. It asks hypothetical questions such as whether an object would fit in a specified place. Accuracy rises in **12 of 13 modular pairs**, by up to **23.8 points**, and never falls in the main table. Configuration questions from the same benchmark, concerning the scene as it is, decline in 12 pairs (§4; Table 4).

The authors offer a plausible interpretation: depicting a hypothetical placement can supply the kind of visual representation that question needs. That is a different use from preserving a photographed stool's actual color. The study does not isolate question type as the cause of the gains, and the matched compatibility control remains uncertain.

The question's purpose therefore matters alongside the helper's fidelity. The same generation tool can face quite different requirements when depicting a possibility and when enlarging existing evidence.

### More calls and more text have different effects

In the extended edit experiment, Qwen3-VL 8B and FLUX.1-Kontext keep **one hint fixed per question**. The first generation edits the first original; every later call edits the previous helper. At each checkpoint the model answers afresh using the originals and only the latest helper (Appendix C.7).

The following scores come from that experiment's rerun baseline, using the same **1,425 ChartQAPro, 1,000 MathVista and 400 ERQA questions** at every checkpoint:

| Generator calls | ChartQAPro | MathVista | ERQA |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 39.16% | 67.70% | 42.50% |
| 2 | 37.89% | 66.60% | 44.00% |
| 10 | 37.40% | 66.30% | 45.50% |

Charts and MathVista remain below their no-generation baselines at every evaluated checkpoint. ERQA ends **three points above baseline**, with fluctuations along the way. Each generator call takes approximately **47–49 seconds on an L40 GPU**, excluding planning and answering. This is repeated editing under a fixed hint, rather than adaptive replanning after each answer (Appendix C.7; Table 10).

Text reasoning needs its own baseline too. On ChartQAPro, enabling chain-of-thought raises Qwen's no-generation accuracy from **40.8% to 56.6%**. Adding a FLUX helper in that thinking condition gives **51.2%**, a loss of **5.4 points** against the appropriate thinking baseline. Its improvement over 40.8% would not establish that the image helped (§5; Table 5).

The experiments now distinguish the plan, the generated artifact and the model's final use of it. A larger reasoning trace or an extra picture earns its value through the answer it changes.

## Core Concepts

**A clear helper is still a claim about the source.** A crisp white stool does not establish that the photographed stool was white. The useful question is whether the auxiliary image retains the detail needed for this answer.

**Reusing pixels and synthesizing a scene have different guarantees.** A crop points back to the original region. A generated helper reconstructs content and may alter attributes. Hypothetical placement questions can benefit from a depiction, while factual color or label questions need their evidence preserved.

**The comparison has to hold the plan fixed.** A direct answer and a two-turn hint-plus-image answer change several things. Comparing hint only, a repeated original and a generated helper isolates the helper's contribution more closely. A correct final answer alone cannot show which extra step was useful.

These distinctions turn “more visual reasoning” into a testable claim about a particular question and protocol.

## The Insight

Additional pixels are useful when they preserve or expose what the question needs. In the stool example, the helper changes the decisive attribute. In the VSTAR controls, retaining original pixels avoids much of the loss observed with generated edits.

A synthetic depiction can serve another purpose, as the hypothetical compatibility results suggest. Its role should follow the question, and its benefit should be measured against the same planning context.

## A Critical Reading

The strongest evidence is the matched VSTAR comparison, with original inputs, hint and answer instructions held fixed. The crop and preservation-hint controls make the fidelity question more concrete. The broad 130-entry comparison is useful coverage, but its aggregate decline includes changes to planning as well as generation.

Primary evaluation tables report point accuracies under specified inference settings. The paired control intervals resample questions 2,000 times; they quantify uncertainty for those matched questions, not variation across all model choices and generator seeds. The small failure-annotation sample helps identify candidate explanations, without establishing population-level causal percentages.

Some answer scoring uses rules and some uses a model judge. Appendix C.2 reports **37% judge accuracy versus 39% human accuracy** on a 100-instance audit of Qwen's direct predictions. Similar aggregate scores do not reveal per-question agreement. Later matched and scaling experiments use a different judge variant, so the audit should not be read as exhaustive validation of every condition.

Implementation also affects outcomes. The evaluated multi-turn approach performs better than an alternative cached-prefix implementation, and is used in the main experiments. The authors leave the source of that gap unresolved; it is not evidence of an inherent limitation of caching (Appendix C.3).

The study holds selected reasoners fixed and focuses on static image question answering. Trained reasoning specialists, adaptive long text–image interactions, video or simulator generation and non-QA agent tasks remain outside these results. The evidence supports testing helper fidelity and contribution in the stated settings, while leaving broader visual reasoning methods open (Appendix D).

## What You Can Use

For a question about the stool's color, retain the original evidence and try a grounded crop when it addresses the difficulty. If adding a generated helper, compare answers with the same hint and originals, counting both newly introduced errors and corrected ones.

For a hypothetical placement question, a generated depiction may serve a different role. Check whether it respects the supplied constraints and improves answers on that task. The paper's useful habit is to evaluate the helper as part of the evidence chain, rather than credit it merely because the final picture is clearer or the final answer is correct.

## 翻译

### 原图留下，再添一张辅助图

能看图回答问题的**视觉语言模型**，先拿到问题与原始图片。
它暂缓回答，用一两句话描述需要什么辅助图，例如裁剪、旋转或加注释（第2节）。

生成器接着产出一张图；只接受一张输入的生成器编辑第一张原图，能接受多张的则使用全部输入。
最终回答轮次里，原图、问题、模型自己的提示和生成辅助图一起进入上下文。

论文把直接回答称为“纯文本推理”，但这个基线**照样看得到原图**。
这里说的是没有生成视觉中间步骤，并非没有视觉输入。

![论文开篇的凳子示例：蓝凳被画成白凳，以及研究报告的辅助图准确率比较。](/arxiv-visuals/arxiv-generated-images-visual-reasoning-evidence/overview.png)

*原论文图1上半部分。凳子是定性示例，柱图汇总模块化流程的结果；后面的匹配对照使用另行说明的受控协议。*

回到凳子，理想辅助图应该把现有物体放大，同时保留它的颜色。
测试流程却让生成系统重新合成一张图，能否保住问题所需的证据，就成了任务的一部分。

### 先比较完整流程，再问是哪一步出了问题

研究把Qwen3-VL 4B、Qwen3-VL 8B与GLM-4.6V Flash分别配上四种生成器，得到12组，再加Gemini 3.0 Flash与Nano-Banana，合计13组。
BAGEL与Janus-Pro-7B另作统一模型测试，由同一个模型理解与生成图像（第3节）。

九个基准得到**十类任务结果**，因为RoboSpatial分成兼容性与配置问题。
**13组流程乘10类任务，共130项比较，106项下降**，报告的平均变化是**−3.7个百分点**。
这里数的是模型组合与任务的分数格子，不是130个单独问题；平均也不是把所有基准题目混合计算的准确率（第4节；表3–4）。

取Qwen3-VL 8B搭配FLUX.1-Kontext这一组，主表结果如下：

| 任务 | 根据原图直接回答 | 提示加生成辅助图 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| VSTAR视觉搜索 | 81.6% | 69.6% |
| MathVista | 61.2% | 57.3% |
| RoboSpatial兼容性 | 61.6% | 70.5% |

VSTAR问高分辨率图片里的小物体，凳子的例子就属于这类困难。
13组模块化组合在这里全部下降，幅度从**3.8到31.3个百分点**。
主比较同时改变了规划过程和辅助图，测到的是整条流程的效果（第4节）。

两个统一模型也都在十类任务中的八类下降，但各自的增益不同：BAGEL在MathVista与RoboSpatial兼容性上提高，Janus在VisualPuzzles与Rover-TG上提高。
这些结果描述所测试的实现，还不能代表所有可能的统一推理系统。

### 可能出错的，是两件不同的事

作者在每个抽样基准里标注**10–20个失败例子**，判断错误进入哪一步。
这一标注子集中，**66%是提示有问题，28%是生成有问题，6%是最终推断有问题**。
这些比例属于选出的失败样本，还不是全部基准错误的因果占比（第5节；附录C.4）。

提示可能指向错误区域，或要求一种无助于回答的变换。
生成器也可能把提示已经找对的细节丢掉；图表和小物体搜索尤其依赖颜色、标签和微小对象。

凳子的示例把后一种问题摆出来：放大请求得到了一张物体已经变样的新图。
要分清生成图与前面规划的作用，下一步就得把规划固定住。

### 回答轮次里，只换辅助内容

在**191个VSTAR问题**上，Qwen3-VL 8B拿到相同原图、相同规划提示、相同两轮对话结构和最后指令。
回答轮次分别不加图、再放一份第一张原图，或放FLUX.1-Kontext生成的辅助图，答案使用贪心解码（附录C.8；表11）。

| 同一提示之后的辅助条件 | 准确率 |
| --- | ---: |
| 只有提示 | 60.21% |
| 再放一份原图 | 58.64% |
| 生成辅助图 | 49.74% |

相对只有提示，生成图把**28个正确答案变错，8个错误答案变对**。
净损失为**10.47个百分点**，配对95%自助抽样区间为**−16.75到−4.71**；相对重复原图，损失为**8.90个百分点**，区间为**−14.66到−2.62**（第5节；附录C.8）。

这组对照是在规划之后回答，要与它自己的“只有提示”基线比较，不能混用前面主表里规划之前直接回答的分数。
它在这套协议下单独看出了生成辅助图带来的额外损失。

另两类受控任务没有同样明确。
RoboSpatial兼容性相对只有提示提高**1.90个百分点**，区间跨过零；ChartQAPro变化为**−0.07个百分点**，区间也跨过零。
VSTAR的结果还不能证明每类任务都有相同的图像单独效应（附录C.8）。

### 要求裁剪时，可以直接留住原来的像素

如果提示明确要求裁剪或放大，Qwen根据第一张原图与固定提示定位区域，不接触参考答案。
实验裁出原始像素并缩放，没有重新生成一个替代场景（第5节；附录C.8）。

同一批**43个符合条件的VSTAR问题**，只有提示得**69.77%**，原像素裁剪得**72.09%**，生成辅助图得**51.16%**。
裁剪比生成高**20.93个百分点**，95%区间为**4.65–37.21**；这个大差距是对生成图而言，裁剪分数仍接近只有提示的分数。

问凳子颜色时，这个区别很实在：放大原像素，还能追溯照片里的物体；重新生成，却可能画出一个清楚但颜色已经改变的物体。

明确要求保真，也没有消除观察到的损失。
提示要求保留相关物体、属性、文字、数字与空间关系后，VSTAR只有提示为**62.83%**，加生成图为**52.88%**，下降**9.95个百分点**，配对区间为**−15.18到−4.19**（表11）。

裁剪结论的范围也要守住。
这里只有43题，另外RoboSpatial兼容性的裁剪子集为21题，ChartQAPro只有2题；还不能据此说所有视觉问题都该用裁剪。

### 有些题目，问的是尚未存在的场景

RoboSpatial兼容性是主表最明显的正例，问题类似“把一件物体放到指定地方，放得下吗”。
13组模块化组合里，**12组提高，最多23.8个百分点**，没有一组下降；同一基准中询问现有场景配置的问题，却有12组下降（第4节；表4）。

作者给出一个可能解释：画出假想摆放，也许更接近这类问题需要的视觉表示。
这与保留照片里凳子的真实颜色，承担着不同工作；研究没有单独隔离题型来证明增益原因，匹配兼容性对照的结果也仍有不确定性。

所以，辅助图是否保真之外，还要看问题想让它做什么。
描绘一种可能，与放大已经存在的证据，对同一生成工具提出了不同要求。

### 多画几轮，与多想一段，效果也不同

扩展编辑实验使用Qwen3-VL 8B与FLUX.1-Kontext，每题**只产生一个固定提示**。
第一轮编辑第一张原图，之后编辑上一轮辅助图；每个检查点都重新回答，只看原图和最新辅助图（附录C.7）。

下表来自这项实验重跑的基线，各检查点使用同样的**1,425道ChartQAPro、1,000道MathVista和400道ERQA题**：

| 生成器调用数 | ChartQAPro | MathVista | ERQA |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | 39.16% | 67.70% | 42.50% |
| 2 | 37.89% | 66.60% | 44.00% |
| 10 | 37.40% | 66.30% | 45.50% |

图表和MathVista在每个受测检查点都低于不生成基线；ERQA最后比基线高**3个百分点**，中途上下波动。
一次生成在L40上约需**47–49秒**，还没算规划和回答。
这测的是固定提示下重复编辑，并不是每次回答后重新规划下一张图（附录C.7；表10）。

文字推理也要找对基线。
ChartQAPro打开逐步推理后，不生成图的准确率从**40.8%升到56.6%**；同样思考设置下再加FLUX辅助图，为**51.2%**，相对正确的思考基线低**5.4个百分点**。
只拿51.2%与40.8%比较，还不能证明那张图帮了忙（第5节；表5）。

这些实验终于把规划、生成的东西，以及最后如何使用它拆开来检查。
更长的推理或额外图片，需要由它改变的答案来证明价值。

## 核心概念

**画得清楚的辅助图，仍然只是在描述来源。**
白凳画得再清楚，也没证明照片里的凳子原本就是白色。
要问的是，它有没有保留这道题所需的细节。

**复用像素与合成场景，能保证的事情不同。**
裁剪还能指回原来的区域，生成则会重构内容，可能改变属性。
假想摆放可以借助描绘，真实颜色与已有标签则需要留住证据。

**比较时，要把规划固定住。**
直接回答与两轮提示加图，同时改变了几件事；比较只有提示、重复原图和生成图，才能更接近辅助图自己的贡献。
最后答对一次，也不能单独说明哪个新增步骤有用。

守住这些区别，“更多视觉推理”就能变成针对具体问题和协议的可检验说法。

## 洞见

新增像素只有留住或显出题目需要的信息，才有用。
凳子示例里，辅助图改掉了决定答案的属性；VSTAR对照里，保留原像素避免了生成编辑造成的大部分损失。

合成图也可能承担另一种工作，假想兼容性的结果提供了线索。
它的角色要跟着问题走，收益则需要在相同规划上下文下测出来。

## 博导审稿

最有力的证据，是固定原始输入、提示和回答指令的VSTAR配对对照，裁剪与保真提示控制又把细节保留的问题落到了实处。
130格主比较提供了覆盖面，但整体下降里还包含规划过程的变化。

主表给的是特定推理设置下的点准确率；配对区间对问题重复抽样2,000次，测的是这些配对题目的不确定性，没有涵盖所有模型选择和生成随机性的变化。
小规模失败标注适合提出解释线索，还不能给出总体错误的因果比例。

部分评分用规则，部分用模型判别器。
附录C.2对Qwen直接回答的100例审计，报告模型评分**37%**、人工**39%**，接近的总体分数仍没说明逐题一致率。
后来的匹配对照与扩展实验还使用另一判别器版本，不能把这次审计当作所有条件的完整验证。

实现也会影响结果。
受测多轮方式比另一种缓存前缀方式更好，因此主实验采用前者；作者尚未查清差距来源，这不是缓存本身必然有局限的证据（附录C.3）。

研究固定了所选推理模型，范围是静态图片问答。
专门训练的推理模型、自适应长文字与图像交互、视频或模拟器生成，以及非问答智能体任务，都超出这里的结果。
证据支持在指定设置里检查辅助图的保真与贡献，更广的视觉推理方法仍是开放问题（附录D）。

## 启发

问凳子颜色时，可以保留原始证据，困难确实来自局部细节时，试一个定位到原图的裁剪。
若加入生成辅助图，就在同样提示与原图下比较答案，同时数清新引入了多少错误、修正了多少错误。

换成假想摆放问题，生成描绘可能承担别的作用，还要检查它是否遵守给定约束、是否提高这类题的答案准确率。
论文留下的习惯，是把辅助图放进证据链里评估，不能只因最后图更清楚、或最后答对了，就把功劳归给它。
