## The Gap

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR)—the post-training engine powering reasoning frontiers like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1—is bounded by a brutal economic constraint: **sampling long chain-of-thought rollout tokens from large models is astronomically expensive**. To amortize generation costs across cluster nodes, practitioners dramatically scale up the rollout batch size $B$, pairing it with the classical square-root learning-rate scaling rule:

$$\eta \propto \sqrt{B}$$

In idealized single-step rollouts, this rule appears to preserve per-sample optimization progress. However, in practical multi-step optimization runs (e.g., standard GRPO or PPO over multiple optimizer epochs), training curves exhibit an exasperating paradox:

1. **The Progress Penalty**: When batch size is scaled up, models require significantly *more* total environment samples to reach the same math or code reasoning benchmark threshold.
2. **The Scheduled Collapse**: Despite learning slower per sample, large-batch runs do not survive longer—they crash and experience policy collapse at nearly the *exact same cumulative sample count* as smaller batch runs.

Why do larger rollout batches simultaneously learn slower yet fail on schedule?

```
   THE MULTI-STEP RLVR SCALING PARADOX

   Rollout Batch B scaled by 16x (with lr * sqrt(16) = lr * 4):
   
   Expectation: Same per-sample progress; linear wall-clock speedup.
   
   Reality (The Two-Clock Divergence):
   +-----------------------------------------------------------+
   | Clock 1 (The Progress Clock - Optimization Geometry)      |
   | - Takes fewer, wider steps into high-curvature zones      |
   | - Second-order Taylor terms destroy progress efficiency   |
   | ===> Requires 2x MORE samples to hit target benchmark!   |
   +-----------------------------------------------------------+
   | Clock 2 (The Failure Clock - Policy Drift Dynamics)       |
   | - Square-root lr preserves cumulative first-order drift   |
   | - Cumulative policy drift hits the cliff at sample N*     |
   | ===> COLLAPSES ON THE EXACT SAME SCHEDULE!               |
   +-----------------------------------------------------------+
   Net Result: Policy crashes BEFORE reaching target accuracy!
```

## The Increment

**One sentence**: By decomposing multi-step RLVR optimization into a curvature-penalized Progress Clock and a drift-invariant Failure Clock, the authors reveal why square-root scaling breaks down beyond a critical batch size and introduce a two-stage batch schedule that achieves both maximum sample efficiency and robust training stability.

### Core Mechanism

The authors model the policy gradient landscape under multi-step off-policy updates using a local quadratic expansion:

1. **The Progress Clock & Curvature Loss**:
   - The first-order parameter drift per sample scales with $\eta / \sqrt{B} \approx \text{const}$.
   - However, the second-order curvature penalty scales with $\eta^2 \approx B$.
   - Because a batch size of $B$ takes $1/B$ as many optimizer steps, each individual step is substantially longer. The quadratic curvature penalty eats up the objective gain, establishing a **learning-rate-dependent critical batch size ($B^*$)**.
   - Training with $B > B^*$ suffers from severe diminishing returns in sample efficiency.
2. **The Failure Clock & Drift Invariance**:
   - Policy collapse in RLVR (entropy collapse, reward hacking, or ratio explosion) is governed by cumulative parameter drift away from the reference checkpoint $\pi_0$.
   - Because the square-root rule holds expected first-order drift per sample approximately constant, the policy reaches the critical drift instability threshold at an **invariant cumulative sample count**, regardless of batch size.
3. **Controlled Empirical Evidence on GRPO**:
   - Validated on Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B and 7B across a $16\times$ batch sweep.
   - PPO/GRPO ratio clipping cushions intermediate steps and delays collapse, but completely fails to close the curvature-driven progress gap.
4. **The Two-Stage RLVR Schedule**:
   - To reconcile sample efficiency and stability, the authors derive a clean operational schedule:
     - **Stage 1 (Rapid Ascent)**: Start with $B < B^*$ (small batch, conservative lr) where curvature loss is minimal, driving rapid early accuracy gains.
     - **Stage 2 (Stability Transit)**: Switch to a larger batch size before approaching the critical drift threshold of the Failure Clock, stabilizing the policy through asymptotic convergence.

```
   THE DUAL DYNAMICS OF MULTI-STEP RLVR

   Optimization Space
           ^
           |     High-Curvature Valley
           |           \   /
           |            \_/  <-- Large Batch Step (Loses to curvature!)
           |             *
           |           /   \
           |          *     *  <-- Small Batch Steps (Follows curvature smoothly)
           |
           +---------------------------------------------> Sample Count
                                                |
                                                v
                                    [Failure Clock Deadline]
                               Policy Drift Hits Catastrophic Cliff
```

### Key Concepts

- **Progress Clock**: The physical rate of metric improvement per consumed rollout sample, dictated by the interplay between first-order gradients and second-order Hessian curvature.
- **Failure Clock**: The invariant sample horizon at which cumulative parameter drift breaches the policy's stable exploration basin, inducing entropy collapse.
- **Critical Batch Size ($B^*$)**: The operational ceiling for rollout batch size beyond which second-order curvature losses outpace first-order gradient improvements.

## Takeaways

- Do not assume square-root learning rate scaling maintains sample efficiency in multi-step RLVR; large batches bleed progress to loss curvature.
- PPO/GRPO ratio clipping can delay policy collapse, but it cannot fix the fundamental curvature penalty of oversized batches.
- Implement a two-stage batch schedule: explore and ascend with sub-critical batch sizes, then expand batch size to safely navigate late-stage convergence.

## 缺口

以 DeepSeek-R1 与 OpenAI o1 为代表的**带可验证奖励强化学习（RLVR）**，是当前大模型实现深度推理跃升的绝对主战场。
然而，RLVR 的训练面临着极度严苛的显存与算力枷锁：让大模型自发生成成千上万条数千 Token 的长思维链（Chain-of-Thought Rollout），开销高昂到令人咋舌。为了摊薄通信与采样成本，算法工程师通常会采取业界标准的经验法则——大幅提高 Rollout 批次大小（Batch Size, $B$），并配合**平方根学习率缩放公式（Square-Root Scaling Rule）**：

$$\eta \propto \sqrt{B}$$

在单步更新的理想化设定下，该规则似乎能保持单位样本的优化进度。但在工业界真实的多步训练（Multi-step Training，即每个 Rollout 批次进行多轮梯度更新）实践中，研究者却普遍撞上了一个令人费解的诡异悖论：

1. **进展惩罚（越学越慢）**：将 Batch Size 放大后，模型达到相同的数学或代码评测基准，所需的总采样数据量反而**成倍激增**，样本利用效率大幅缩水。
2. **准时崩盘（毫不延寿）**：尽管大批次每步学得更慢，但它遭遇策略瓦解、熵值坍塌（Policy Collapse）的**总样本数节点却分毫不差地准时降临**！

为什么大批次在多步 RLVR 中学得更慢，却依然在相同的倒计时下准时暴毙？

```
   多步 RLVR 批次缩放的「双时钟」背离悖论

   Rollout Batch 放大 16 倍（学习率按 sqrt(16)=4 倍放大）：
   
   理论美好预期：单位样本学习进度恒定，算力利用率线性拉满。
   
   残酷实测真相（双时钟机制背道而驰）：
   +-----------------------------------------------------------+
   | 时钟 1：进展时钟（Progress Clock - 优化曲率几何）         |
   | - 步数减少但步长拉大，陷入损失函数的高阶强曲率深谷        |
   | - 二阶泰勒项剧烈吞噬梯度收益                              |
   | ===> 达到相同准确率所需的真实采样量激增 2 倍！            |
   +-----------------------------------------------------------+
   | 时钟 2：失败时钟（Failure Clock - 参数漂移稳定性）        |
   | - 平方根法则恰好锁定了单位样本的一阶累计漂移量            |
   | - 参数偏离基座模型的总距离在第 N* 个样本处准时触碰悬崖    |
   +-----------------------------------------------------------+
   灾难后果：在进展时钟还没来得及跑出高分之前，失败时钟已经敲响了丧钟！
```

## 增量

**一句话**: 本文提出了「双时钟框架（Two-Clock Framework）」，从损失曲率的「进展时钟」与参数漂移的「失败时钟」两个维度解构了多步 RLVR 的缩放动力学，揭示了平方根规则在超过临界批次后样本效率劣化的本质，并据此设计了兼顾样本效率与长程稳定性的「先小后大两阶段批次调度法则」。

### 核心机制

研究团队基于局部二次曲面（Local Quadratic Model）展开了精密推导，将原本混淆的训练进程清晰拆解为走时独立的两个时钟：

1. **进展时钟与高阶曲率惩罚**：
   - 沿用平方根规则时，单位样本的一阶梯度位移期望 $\eta / \sqrt{B} \approx \text{常数}$。
   - 然而，损失函数的二阶海森曲率（Hessian Curvature）惩罚正比于 $\eta^2 \approx B$。
   - 大 Batch 使得优化步数减少、单步更新跨度剧烈增大，导致每一步都被二阶非线性曲率大幅「反噬」，由此定义了**依赖于学习率的「临界批次大小（Critical Batch Size, $B^*$）」**。当 $B > B^*$ 时，单位样本的有效信息增益呈现断崖式下跌。
2. **失败时钟与一阶漂移恒定性**：
   - RLVR 中策略崩溃（如比率爆炸、熵值归零）的根源，是策略权重偏离原始参考模型 $\pi_0$ 累积了一阶漂移（First-order Drift）。
   - 因为平方根学习率严格保持了一阶累计漂移对样本量的恒定性，导致**不论 Batch 设多大，模型都会在几乎完全相同的总样本消耗量处撞上崩溃阈值**。
3. **GRPO 严格受控实验验证**：
   - 在 Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B 与 7B 模型上完成了跨度达 $16\times$ 的完整批次网格实验。
   - 实验揭示：传统 PPO/GRPO 的 Ratio Clipping 只能稍微推迟失败时钟的响铃时间，却完全无法弥合大批次因曲率造成的进展落后。
4. **两阶段动态批次调度器（Two-Stage Schedule）**：
   - **第一阶段（高效爬坡）**：在训练前期采用低于临界批次的较小 Batch（$B < B^*$），将损失曲率惩罚降至最低，用极高样本效率快速拉升推理准度。
   - **第二阶段（平稳滑翔）**：在累积样本逼近失败时钟红线之前，动态将 Batch 放大、平抑局部扰动，安全护航模型平稳收敛至全局最优。

```
   多步 RLVR 优化曲面与双时钟动力学示意图

   优化目标流形
        ^
        |      强非线性高阶曲率深谷
        |            \   /
        |             \_/   <-- 大 Batch 巨幅跨步（被曲率惩罚大幅吞噬！）
        |              *
        |            /   \
        |           *     * <-- 小 Batch 连续平滑小步（紧贴曲率优雅上升）
        |
        +---------------------------------------------> 累积消耗样本量
                                             |
                                             v
                                  [失败时钟不可逾越的红线]
                            一阶参数漂移超标，策略陷入熵坍塌崩盘
```

### 关键概念

- **进展时钟（Progress Clock）**：衡量模型能力指标随采样量增长的真实上升速率，由一阶梯度推力与二阶海森曲率阻力的博弈决定。
- **失败时钟（Failure Clock）**：衡量策略网络偏离基座权重发生不可逆崩溃的倒计时，由一阶参数漂移的累积量主导。
- **临界批次大小（Critical Batch Size, $B^*$）**：多步 RLVR 中样本效率由高效跃迁至迟钝的拐点；超过此阈值，增大 Batch 必然导致单位样本学习效率缩水。

## 行囊 (Takeaways)

- 在多步 RLVR 中，切忌盲目相信平方根学习率缩放能保住大批次的样本效率；过大的 Batch 必然在高阶曲率面前严重丢分。
- Ratio Clipping 只能稍微延缓崩溃发生的时间，治标不治本，无法解决进展落后的硬伤。
- 采用「前期小批次冲锋爬坡、后期大批次稳定收敛」的两阶段调度机制，是目前破解 RLVR 算力浪费的最优解。
