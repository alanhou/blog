**Paper**: [2611.33145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33145) — Lost on Campus: Evaluating Embodied Spatial Reasoning of Vision-Language Models in the Wild\
**Authors**: Zehan Zheng, Yanyuan Chen, Deming Li, Yutao Tang, Jianwen Xie, Alan Yuille, Jieneng Chen, Cheng Peng\
**Category**: cs.CV\
**Reading scope**: Original arXiv v1 HTML, including all five main sections, Limitations and Appendices A–D. Tables 1–17 were checked through original HTML cells. Figures 1, 2, 4 and 5 were visually inspected. Bibliographic references, external papers and implementation were not independently audited; the experiments were not reproduced.

## The Problem

Imagine a teaching example: give an AI a photo of a campus building's entrance and ask it to reach the place where that photo was taken. It can recognize the building on its right. It still has to choose a turn, move along the path, recognize its changing position and stop at the goal.

Those decisions expose a gap that a correct answer about a photograph can leave hidden. Earlier spatial benchmarks often ask models to inspect images or videos; many interactive benchmarks use indoor scenes. The authors argue that familiar indoor associations can help a model find an object without requiring precise geometry. Their campus test asks how well spatial judgments survive repeated movement in a larger outdoor setting (§§1–2).

**Lost on Campus is an evaluation resource.** It combines navigation through a reconstructed campus with spatial questions drawn from navigation trajectories. The contribution is a way to observe failures and investigate them, with measured results for existing models (§§1 and 3).

## The Explanation

### Let each move change what the model sees

The building in our example becomes part of a repeatable simulation. The team collected more than 50,000 calibrated frames, covering roughly 200,000 square metres across 15 scenes: 11 around buildings and four around intersections. Accurate GPS measurements anchor the reconstruction's metre scale (§3.1).

They use **3D Gaussian Splatting**, a reconstruction method that renders a photographed scene from new camera positions, with modifications for appearance and geometry. The environment can therefore show what a camera would see after a move. These are rendered observations from static campus captures. The evaluation does not involve driving a physical robot around campus.

At a decision, the model sees front, left and right images, each at 512 × 512 pixels, plus the goal image. It receives an eight-step sliding history of observations and actions. It can move forward or backward by one metre, turn left or right by 15 or 45 degrees, or stop. A decision may contain several primitive actions before the next observation; an episode is capped at 100 steps (§4; Appendix C).

For the entrance example, a right turn changes the view used for the next decision. The model must keep updating where it is, rather than answer once and leave the consequences untested.

*Original Figure 1. The same reconstructed environment supports action evaluation and questions grounded in navigation trajectories.*

### Ask smaller questions about the same kind of journey

The general navigation test has **1,500 cases**, 100 per scene. Start and goal camera positions are 5–25 metres apart, with sampling balanced across distance intervals. A separate instruction setting has **255 harder cases**, including longer journeys and intersections (§3.2; Appendix A.2).

The diagnostic test contains **6,000 question pairs**, 1,000 for each of six tasks, constructed from pooled model navigation rollouts. Two questions make the connection especially clear. Given the entrance view and a proposed move, can the model pick the correct future image from four choices? Given a ground-level view and an overhead map, can it locate itself or the goal on that map?

The other probes ask models to infer actions from changing images, judge distance changes, choose an intermediate viewpoint toward the goal, and track an object's relative position as the camera moves. These are complementary probes of the perception–action loop, rather than six independent parts that exhaust spatial reasoning (§3.2; Appendix A.3).

Questions can show where the model struggles. Navigation then asks whether those judgments work together over a whole journey.

### Reaching the neighbourhood and stopping are different outcomes

The navigation score distinguishes **success rate (SR)**, stopping within the goal radius, from **oracle success rate (OSR)**, entering that radius at any point during the journey. “Oracle” here means the evaluator checks the entire recorded trajectory; it does not mean the model receives extra help.

On the same 1,500 general cases, the strongest evaluated model, Gemini-3-Flash, reports (§4.1; Table 2):

| Goal radius | Stopped within the radius | Entered the radius at some point |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2.5 m | 25.73% | 36.13% |
| 5 m | 49.60% | 67.73% |

In our example, a model can pass near the entrance, continue walking and eventually stop elsewhere. The stricter radius also exposes how much precision matters. None of the evaluated open-source models exceeds 11% success at 2.5 metres.

The authors additionally optimize the final camera pose after the model has stopped, keeping its executed action sequence unchanged. This adds **10.00 percentage points** to Gemini-3-Flash's strict success score. That gain comes from an additional post-episode alignment procedure and supports investigating endpoint precision separately (§4.1; Table 3).

### The efficiency ranking can reverse at the tighter goal

Qwen3.5-27B has the highest **path-weighted success** at the five-metre radius: **31.91%**, slightly above Gemini-3-Flash's **31.16%**. This measure discounts successful routes when they are inefficient. Yet Qwen's success at 2.5 metres is **9.93%**, compared with Gemini's **25.73%** (§4.1; Table 2).

An encouraging efficiency score at a loose radius does not settle whether the model can finish precisely. The paper reports a 95% bootstrap confidence interval of **8.47–11.47%** for Qwen's 9.93% strict success on the full benchmark; that describes uncertainty from resampling these cases (Appendix D.2; Table 14).

The diagnostic questions help narrow the next investigation. Gemini's average diagnostic score is 63.13%, but its map-localization accuracy is only **41.7%** on a four-choice task whose chance level is 25%. Its future-view accuracy is **61.9%**, also with 25% chance. Model rankings broadly align across diagnostic scores and navigation at the five-metre radius, but these different measurements cannot substitute for each other (§§4.2–4.3; Table 4).

### Supply a missing piece and observe what changes

To investigate localization, the authors keep Qwen3.5-27B and a scene-balanced subset of **150 episodes** fixed, then change the spatial information it receives. A map with only the goal marked adds **1.34 points** to strict success. A map giving the exact goal, current position and heading adds **11.34 points** (§4.3; Table 5).

For our entrance, knowing which dot is the destination still leaves the model to work out where it stands and which way it faces. Supplying all of those quantities makes navigation easier. The experiment supports localization as a contributor to failure, while measuring an assisted condition rather than an acquired skill.

A second intervention uses the same model and subset. At each decision, the model proposes several action sequences. The environment renders the actual future view for each candidate. Letting the model inspect those views before choosing adds **10.00 points** to strict success. Choosing the candidate with the shortest true distance to the goal adds **26.00 points** (§4.3; Table 6).

The model benefits from seeing action consequences, and the choice among them still matters. The largest gain uses ground-truth distance. These interventions are diagnostic aids; they do not establish that a learned future-view predictor would deliver the same improvement.

Ordinary route guidance is less decisive. On the separate **255 hard cases**, Qwen3.5-27B's five-metre success rises from **3.92% to 8.63%** with a goal-marked overhead map. The map does not supply its exact current pose. For Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, adding a visual route map instead lowers success from **4.71% to 1.96%**. More spatial information can help a model while remaining difficult to use consistently (§4.5; Table 8; Appendix D.5).

The entrance journey now has distinct things to inspect: the frame of reference, the ending condition and the information used to select a move.

## Core Concepts

**A view and a map need a position and a heading between them.** “The building is on my right” describes the camera's current view. An overhead map uses a fixed frame. After a turn, the building can move across the image while staying in the same place on the map. The model must connect those two descriptions to act on map guidance.

**A stopping rule is part of the task.** Passing within five metres of the entrance counts toward OSR; stopping there counts toward SR. Final-view similarity is reported separately. A convincing-looking facade or an efficient route therefore answers only part of the evaluation question.

**An oracle intervention changes the information available.** Exact coordinates and rendered candidate futures let researchers test whether a missing input could help. They are different from a model inferring those quantities reliably from its ordinary observations. The distinction matters when turning a diagnosis into a proposed improvement.

These concepts make the benchmark's useful judgment more specific than a single navigation ranking.

## The Insight

Spatial answers become more informative when their consequences can be checked in the same environment. A model may recognize the entrance and make progress toward it while still failing to locate itself accurately or decide when to stop. Pairing diagnostic questions with navigation helps identify which part of that journey deserves attention.

The measured result applies to this reconstructed campus and evaluation interface. Its value depends on how well those conditions represent the task someone wants to improve.

## A Critical Reading

The paper checks several alternative explanations. It compares ordinary navigation with random exploration and image matching, evaluates specialized embodied models on a matched subset, and tests how reconstructed image quality affects outcomes (Appendices B and D.1–D.4).

For the image-quality check, Qwen3.5-27B uses original or Difix3D+-enhanced observations under matched settings, including 150 navigation episodes. Diagnostic accuracy changes from **59.0% to 61.0%**; strict navigation success changes from **13.3% to 14.0%**, and loose success from **36.7% to 36.0%**. This gives limited support to the authors' interpretation that appearance artifacts are not the main cause of low performance. One enhancement cannot establish that all reconstruction effects have been removed (Appendix B; Table 11).

Answer distributions also matter. On action grounding, removing images leaves Qwen at **44.2%**, while always choosing the most frequent label gives **46.6%**. Uniform random chance is only **16.7%**. Full input reaches **82.5%**, but the majority baseline is essential context for interpreting that improvement (Appendix D.1; Table 12).

The indoor comparison scales start–goal distances, translation steps and goal radii down by five, while keeping other protocol settings fixed. Qwen3.5-27B reaches **59.3%** strict success indoors versus **9.93%** outdoors. Scene content and rendering still differ, so this measures an overall environment difficulty gap; it does not isolate familiar indoor semantics as its cause (Appendix D.3; Table 15).

The remaining scope is substantial: one campus, static captures, predefined motion primitives and diagnostic questions tied to sampled trajectories. The interface does not test changing weather, moving pedestrians or a robot's continuous motor control. The benchmark offers a concrete diagnosis in a repeatable environment, with real-world transfer still to establish (§5, Limitations).

## What You Can Use

For an agent that must reach our entrance, record whether it ever enters the goal region, where it stops and how efficiently it gets there. Keep the same cases and success radius when comparing versions. A spatial-question score can accompany these outcomes, with the actual journey deciding whether the task was completed.

Then investigate a failure with controlled help: supply an exact pose, or show candidate future observations, and see which changes the outcome. Use that result to choose what to build next, then retest with the information the agent will actually have. A map should earn its place through better completed journeys under the intended conditions.

## 问题

先设想一个例子：给AI一张校园教学楼入口的照片，让它走到拍下这张照片的位置。
它能认出右边那栋楼，却还得选对转向、沿路前进、跟上自己位置的变化，最后停在目标处。

只问一张照片里有什么，很容易漏掉后面这些决定。
过去的空间测试常让模型看图片或视频，能互动的测试则多放在室内。
作者认为，熟悉的室内物品关系可能帮模型找到东西，却不必要求它算准几何关系。
校园测试要看的是：到了更大的室外场景，模型的空间判断能不能经得住连续移动（第1–2节）。

**Lost on Campus 是一套评测资源。**
它把校园重建后的导航测试，与从导航轨迹里生成的空间问题接到一起。
论文的贡献，是让现有模型跑一趟，再把失误拆开查清楚（第1、3节）。

## 翻译

### 走一步，画面就得跟着变

例子里的教学楼，先要成为一个能重复测试的模拟场景。
团队采集并校准了超过五万帧画面，覆盖约20万平方米，共15个场景：11个围绕建筑，4个围绕路口。
精确的GPS测量给重建结果定下米制尺度（第3.1节）。

他们采用**三维高斯泼溅**，也就是从实拍画面重建场景，再渲染出新相机位置所见的画面，并对外观和几何部分做了调整。
这样，相机前进一步，环境就能给出对应的新图。
测试中的观察来自静态校园素材的重建与渲染，没有让实体机器人在校园里跑。

每次决定前，模型拿到前、左、右三个方向的画面，每张512 × 512像素，再加目标照片。
它还拿到最近八步的观察与动作历史。
动作包括前进或后退一米、向左或向右转15度或45度，以及停止。
一次决定可以给出多个基本动作，执行后再看新画面；一轮最多100步（第4节；附录C）。

回到入口例子，右转之后看到的东西会变。
模型得根据新图继续更新自己的位置，前一个判断的后果才能接受检验。

![Lost on Campus 原始总览图：重建校园中的导航，与六类轨迹相关的空间诊断题相连。](/arxiv-visuals/arxiv-lost-on-campus-spatial-reasoning/overview.png)

*原论文图1。同一套重建环境，既能检查动作结果，也能提供与导航轨迹相关的空间问题。*

### 从同一类路程里拆出小问题

常规导航有**1,500个案例**，每个场景100个。
起点和目标相机位置相距5–25米，抽样时让各距离区间尽量均衡。
另外还有**255个较难案例**，包含更远的路程和路口，用来测试不同形式的指引（第3.2节；附录A.2）。

诊断题共有**6,000对问答**，六类任务各1,000题，素材来自多个模型跑出的导航轨迹。
其中两类最容易看出与入口例子的联系：给当前画面和一组动作，能不能从四张候选图里选出动作之后的画面？给地面视角和俯视地图，能不能找出自己或目标在地图上的位置？

其余题目分别检查：能否从画面变化认出动作、判断离目标越来越近还是越来越远、选出通向目标的中间位置，以及在相机移动后记住某个物体相对自己在哪里。
这六类题从不同角度检查观察与动作的衔接，彼此并非完全独立，也不穷尽空间推理（第3.2节；附录A.3）。

问答能暴露局部短板。
导航还要看这些判断能不能接起来，把一趟路走完。

### 走近过，与停下来，是两件事

导航区分两个分数：**成功率SR**要求模型停在目标半径内；**经过成功率OSR**只看整条轨迹有没有进入过这个范围。
OSR原名中的“oracle”，在这里指评测者检查完整轨迹，并没有额外帮模型指路。

在同一批1,500个常规案例里，受测表现最好的Gemini-3-Flash得到以下结果（第4.1节；表2）：

| 目标半径 | 最后停在范围内 | 途中曾进入范围 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2.5米 | 25.73% | 36.13% |
| 5米 | 49.60% | 67.73% |

回到教学楼入口，模型可能已经从附近经过，又继续向前，最后停在别处。
半径收紧之后，精度的问题也更明显。
受测的开源模型，没有一个在2.5米半径下超过11%的成功率。

作者还在模型停止后，用三维重建做额外的相机位姿优化，保留已经执行的动作序列。
Gemini-3-Flash的严格成功率因此增加**10.00个百分点**。
这项增益来自一轮结束后追加的对齐程序，也说明终点精度值得单独检查（第4.1节；表3）。

### 宽松半径下的效率领先，未必撑得住严格终点

Qwen3.5-27B在五米半径下的**路径加权成功率**最高，为**31.91%**，略高于Gemini-3-Flash的**31.16%**。
这个分数会给成功但绕路的轨迹打折。
可到了2.5米半径，Qwen的成功率是**9.93%**，Gemini则为**25.73%**（第4.1节；表2）。

宽松半径下不错的效率分，还不能回答模型能否精确收尾。
论文对Qwen的9.93%严格成功率报告了**8.47–11.47%**的95%自助抽样置信区间。
这是对这批案例重复抽样所估计的不确定性（附录D.2；表14）。

诊断题让下一步检查更有方向。
Gemini的六类诊断平均分为63.13%，但地图定位只有**41.7%**，四选一随机猜为25%；预测动作后画面的准确率是**61.9%**，随机猜同样为25%。
模型在诊断平均分与五米导航成功率上的排名大致相关，可这两种分数测的东西不同，不能相互代替（第4.2–4.3节；表4）。

### 补给一块信息，看结果怎么变

定位实验固定Qwen3.5-27B，以及场景均衡的**150轮导航**，改变它拿到的空间信息。
地图只标目标，严格成功率增加**1.34个百分点**；同时给出准确的目标位置、当前位置与朝向，则增加**11.34个百分点**（第4.3节；表5）。

教学楼入口标在地图上，模型仍要认出自己站在哪里、脸朝哪边。
把这些量一起交给它，导航就容易了一些。
实验支持定位错误是失败的一项来源，但测到的是获得额外帮助后的表现。

另一项实验固定同一模型和同一子集。
每次决定时，模型先提出几组动作，环境把每组动作真正会带来的画面渲染出来。
让模型先看这些未来图再选动作，严格成功率增加**10.00个百分点**。
如果直接选出离真实目标最近的候选动作，则增加**26.00个百分点**（第4.3节；表6）。

看得见动作后果能帮忙，如何从候选动作里做选择也仍然重要。
最大增益用到了真实距离。
这些是用来查短板的辅助实验，还不能证明训练出一个未来画面预测器，就能获得同样的提升。

普通指引带来的变化小得多。
在另一批**255个困难案例**里，给Qwen3.5-27B一张只标目标的俯视图，五米成功率从**3.92%升到8.63%**；这张图没有直接给它准确的当前位姿。
而给Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct增加画好的路线图，成功率反而从**4.71%降到1.96%**。
空间信息能帮某些模型，也可能让另一些模型用不顺（第4.5节；表8；附录D.5）。

入口这趟路，现在有了几件可以分开检查的东西：所用的参照系、结束任务的条件，以及选动作时拿到的信息。

## 核心概念

**眼前画面与地图之间，隔着位置和朝向。**
“楼在右边”说的是相机眼前的关系，俯视地图用的是固定参照。
转身之后，楼在画面里移了位置，在地图上却还待在原处。
模型要把这两种描述接起来，地图上的指引才能变成动作。

**何时停下，也是任务的一部分。**
曾经走到入口五米内，可以计入OSR；在那里停下，才计入SR。
最后画面与目标照片的相似度另有指标。
眼前的楼像不像、路有没有绕、终点停没停准，各自回答一部分问题。

**提供真值，会改变模型手里有哪些信息。**
准确坐标和候选未来图，让研究者检查一块缺失信息能不能帮忙。
这与模型自己从普通观察里可靠地推断出这些量，仍有距离。
把诊断结果变成改进方案时，需要守住这个区别。

有了这些区分，评测的用途就能比一张导航排行榜更具体。

## 洞见

把空间答案放回同一个环境，检查它会带来什么动作，答案就更有用。
模型可以认出入口，也朝它走近，却仍然认不准自己在哪里，或者不知道何时停。
把问答和导航配起来，能帮助判断这趟路哪一段值得继续查。

这个结果成立的范围，是论文重建的校园和所用的评测接口。
想拿它改进另一项任务，还得看两边的条件接得有多近。

## 博导审稿

论文检查了几种替代解释：把普通导航与随机探索、图像匹配比较，在相同子集上测专门面向具身任务的模型，也检查重建画质对结果的影响（附录B、D.1–D.4）。

画质实验让Qwen3.5-27B在相同设置下，分别使用原始画面和Difix3D+增强后的画面，其中导航部分有150轮。
诊断准确率从**59.0%变为61.0%**，严格导航成功率从**13.3%变为14.0%**，宽松成功率从**36.7%变为36.0%**。
这些小幅变化，有限地支持作者所说的“外观伪影并非主要原因”。
一种增强方法还不能证明所有重建影响都已排除（附录B；表11）。

答案分布也要看。
动作识别去掉图片，Qwen仍有**44.2%**；始终选最常见的标签，能到**46.6%**；均匀随机猜却只有**16.7%**。
完整输入达到**82.5%**，但理解这项提升时，最常见标签的基线不能漏掉（附录D.1；表12）。

室内对照把起终点距离、平移步长和成功半径缩小为五分之一，其余协议设置保持一致。
Qwen3.5-27B室内严格成功率为**59.3%**，室外为**9.93%**。
两边的场景内容与渲染仍不同，因此测出的是整体环境难度差，尚不能把原因单独归到熟悉的室内语义关系上（附录D.3；表15）。

边界仍然明显：一所校园、静态素材、预设动作，以及依赖所抽轨迹的诊断题。
接口没有测试天气变化、行人移动和机器人的连续运动控制。
这套评测能在可重复的环境里给出具体诊断，迁移到现实任务还需要验证（第5节，局限）。

## 启发

让智能体去教学楼入口时，可以同时记下：途中有没有进入目标范围、最后停在哪里、路走得是否有效率。
比较新旧版本时，用同一批案例和同一个成功半径。
空间问答分可以伴随这些结果，但任务有没有完成，还要看实际走出的这一趟。

接着用受控帮助查失误：补上准确位置，或者给出候选动作后的画面，看哪一种会改变结局。
据此选择下一块要做的能力，再撤回额外帮助，用智能体真正能拿到的信息重测。
一张地图有没有用，要落到预期条件下能否更好地完成路程。
